Every claim should be challenged. Here are some of the objections most often raised, with places to find thoughtful answers.

## “Why would a good God send anyone to hell?”

Imagine designing a country with free healthcare, great schools, and honest leaders — but no justice system. Anyone could steal, assault, or murder without consequence. Would it still be a *good* country?

Goodness and justice go hand in hand. God is loving, merciful, and kind — and also perfectly just. At the cross, justice and mercy meet: Jesus took the penalty that sin deserves, so that all who repent and trust in Him can be forgiven.

> God is equally loving and just.

## “Isn’t faith just believing without evidence?”

That's a popular, but mistaken, belief in our modern world. In fact, it's a quick go-to argument that's often used to dismiss any discussion about God.

While it's true that you *could* believe without evidence, Christianity is a faith ***based* on evidence**. The appeal to evidence is as old as Christianity itself. Paul wrote, "if [Jesus] Christ has not been raised" then everything about Christianity is useless (1 Corinthians 15:12-19). That's why the claims of Jesus' resurrection as a [real event in history](/evidence/jesus/) is so critical.

Faith is too often portrayed as being the opposite of scientific thinking. I do have more to say and write on that point (*coming soon*), but I would like you to consider that atheism itself is a faith system. Read more about that in a short blog post I wrote titled <ExternalLink href="https://gilleland.ca/blog/2025/aug/atheism/">Atheism is a belief</ExternalLink>.

View the [resources](/resources/) for links to many of the best evidences for the existence of God.

## “Aren’t all religions basically the same?”

Many people think the world's religions are fundamentally the same and only superficially different from each other. It turns out that the opposite is true. The world's religions are only superficially the same, but they are often fundamentally different from each other.

And there are two critical ways in which Christianity is fundamentally different from all other religious belief systems. First, Christianity depends on a singular [historical event](/evidence/jesus/). Second, Christianity offers a [completely different way of salvation](/respond/what-now/#the-good-news) than all other religions.
