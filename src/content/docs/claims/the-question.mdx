My first claim: **the most important question before you is, “Does God exist?”**

## How can one question be the most important?

The test is simple. Whatever answer you land on — *yes* or *no* — will have a dramatic impact on every other important question in life. Personal and shared. Abstract and concrete.

| If you ask…                         | …your answer depends on whether God exists |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Why am I here?                      | Is there a purpose beyond the one I invent? |
| What makes something right or wrong? | Is morality discovered, or only agreed on? |
| What is a human life worth?         | Are we made in an image, or merely made?   |
| What happens when I die?            | Is death an ending, or a doorway?          |
| Is there any real hope?             | Is anyone there to keep a promise?         |

A “no” and a “yes” lead to very different answers down the whole column. That is what makes this the question beneath the questions.

## The only real objection

There is one serious objection: *“Even if it’s the most important question, it can’t be answered.”*

That is not an objection to the first claim — it is a challenge to the second. And it deserves a careful answer.

<Aside type="tip" title="Don't take my word for it">
These are claims, put forward as statements of fact. Test them. You would do well to examine any claim brought before you.
</Aside>
